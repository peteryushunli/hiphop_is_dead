A phonetic suffix, extended carefully

01 / Read sounds

Use the first CMU dictionary pronunciation consistently. Separate short allowlisted or echoing parentheticals. Move unmarked trailing ad-libs only when punctuation and two unchanged nearby endpoints support the move. Preserve original text and reset at section labels. Unknown words stop the backward scan; they never become invented phones.

02 / Compare endings

Align suffixes of one to six vowel nuclei. Ignore the first onset; retain sounds between nuclei and at the end. Require a stressed vowel in both tails. Extend until the next vowel or added phonetic cost fails the acceptance gate.

03 / Link local rhymes

Compare each line with the previous four lines in its section. Select the link with the most weighted syllables, then rating, then proximity. This permits ABAB. Shared terminal words connect the pattern with no credit. Scan before the shared suffix for novel rhyme. Positive strict rhymes take priority, then repetition, then tentative slants. Unlinked lines remain visible.

04 / Aggregate

Pool links for album mean and median. Sum ratings and novel syllables once per scored incoming link. Repetition-only connections and tentative slants do not enter the main mean or totals. Weighted syllables = syllables × rating / 100. A six-syllable cap is a search limit, not a claim about the artist’s maximum.

Phrase slants use a separate, narrow rule: align at least two syllables around an identical stressed anchor; require a shared intervening consonant; allow a central trailing vowel mismatch when one dictionary vowel is unstressed. Earlier phone costs remain strict. The central mismatch costs 12, final consonant edits cost 4, and stress disagreements cost 4. “Riser” / “guys up” becomes a tentative 80, with the AY Z anchor retained and the ER versus AH P difference penalized. This rule does not infer performed pronunciation.

rating = max(0, 100 − weighted_phone_edit_cost − stress_cost)
weighted_rhyme_syllables = novel_syllables × rating / 100

Consonant substitutions, insertions, and deletions cost 8 points. Selected neighboring vowels cost 16; other vowel edits cost 40. Vowel/consonant substitutions cost 48; stress mismatches cost 4. A strict link must score at least 84, and extending it cannot add more than 16 points of cost. These vowel, stress, and acceptance rules are explicit modeling choices.

Change the assumptions

Settings vary one factor at a time. “Add hooks & bridges” includes labeled verse, chorus, hook, refrain, post-chorus, and bridge sections. Shared words stay uncredited in every scenario. The slant scenario adds selected tentative links to totals; their estimated scores are a separate experiment. Keeping all ad-libs can also retain parenthetical-only lines, changing the denominator.

What the numbers can tell us

The model measures source-text end rhymes. It does not hear delivery, find internal rhymes, establish actual bar boundaries, judge wordplay, or distinguish individual voices inside mixed performer labels. Conditional mean ratings tend to be high because weak matches are filtered out. A higher rating with fewer links is not automatically better.

RhymeZone documents CMU-derived pronunciations and broader near-rhyme discovery from poetry and lyrics. Its public Datamuse API provides phonetic relations, but the documented API score is a search ranking with no interpretable 0–100 meaning. Direct advanced-script access was blocked, so the selected examples support a partial approximation only.
